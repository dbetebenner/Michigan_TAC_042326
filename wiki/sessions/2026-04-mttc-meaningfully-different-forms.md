**Presenters:** Bridgett McDowell (OEE), Nancy Hahn and Heather Klesch (Evaluation Systems –
[[pearson]]), Jackie Thompson (Pearson Professional Assessments) · **Day:** April 23, 2026

The longest session of the meeting — scheduled 1:00–4:00pm with a break. No deck survives in
the corpus; this page is reconstructed from the minutes alone.

## What OEE brought

Two problems under one contract.

**First**, the new Pearson contract requires OEE to "maximize the number of meaningfully
different test forms for each field," and OEE needed the committee to tell it whether its
working definition of *meaningfully different* was defensible.

**Second**, whether to build constructed-response tests for the optional science and social
studies endorsements.

Full threads: [[mttc-meaningfully-different-forms]] and [[mttc-constructed-response-endorsements]].

## The definition problem

OEE's working definition: a single high ceiling on overall item overlap between test forms,
with a slightly lower ceiling on the scorable set, applied uniformly across every field.
Rationale offered was standard practice in educator licensure testing, program integrity, and
legal defensibility. Goal: a minimum of three forms by contract end.

The committee did not accept it, saying the ceiling was far too high to constitute meaningful
difference — and then putting it in terms of the contract's own horizon: a handful of forms
produced over several years, each sharing most of its items with the others, cannot reasonably
be called meaningfully different.

The specific ceilings are held in the restricted layer; see
`restricted/mttc-form-security-detail.md`.

## The constraint that makes it hard

Low-incidence fields do not generate examinees fast enough. Statistical equating needs a
minimum of 100 examinees per form. Re-using a single form raises both security and fairness
risk. Only a small number of forms can be live in any one testing window, and a candidate may
repeat after a fixed interval — those two figures together are the most sensitive detail in the
corpus and are held in the restricted layer.

OEE's response was to sort the certification tests into four groups by incidence. The two
higher-volume groups build a new form once a field reaches the 100-examinee threshold,
differing in equating method. The two lower-volume groups do not equate at all and are
standard-setting driven, with Pearson conducting the standard settings.

Per-group form counts are in the restricted layer. One detail matters publicly: asked why the
permitted form range for the highest-volume group was what it was, the answer was that this was
what the old contract said — not that it followed from Michigan's volumes.

## The validity question underneath

Early in the session the committee asked what inferences OEE can defend for each of these
tests. The answer given was procedural: a passing candidate can be certificated, which allows
a district to interview them.

The committee's response is the most consequential thing said in the session —
[[rec-mttc-define-defensible-inference]]. OEE should be able to answer substantively, not
procedurally: what can you infer when a candidate does *not* reach the cut score, what does
performance mean, and what do MTTC scores represent?

## What the committee advised on forms

- [[rec-mttc-redefine-per-incidence-group]] — one uniform overlap rule across four very different groups is the wrong instrument
- [[rec-mttc-start-with-standard-setting-form]] — begin with a standard-setting form and grow the pool as non-scorable items prove out
- [[rec-mttc-stick-with-100-examinees]] — 100 per form, not 200; waiting buys precision at the cost of forms
- [[rec-mttc-two-year-escalation]] — if 100 cannot be reached in two years, move the field to the next group's process
- [[rec-mttc-reduce-item-overlap]] — target roughly half the working ceiling
- [[rec-mttc-fewer-forms-less-overlap]] — fewer forms with less overlap beats many forms with lots of overlap
- [[rec-mttc-automated-item-generation]] — automated item generation to build the pool
- [[rec-mttc-michigan-specific-incidence-categories]] — define incidence categories for Michigan rather than inheriting them

The two-year escalation rule carries its own reasoning: educator preparation programs will not
change enough in under two years to move the numbers, but after two years the absence of
change is itself information.

## What the committee advised on constructed response

The committee advised **against** building them:
[[rec-mttc-avoid-constructed-response-endorsements]]. "This creates a lot of work, will be
hard to do, and will be expensive; suggest that this isn't necessary."

It offered four alternatives instead —
[[rec-mttc-transcript-review-alternative]],
[[rec-mttc-conjunctive-cut-scores]],
[[rec-mttc-random-standard-prompt]], and
[[rec-mttc-interview-guide-for-districts]].

And it returned to purpose: [[rec-mttc-define-endorsement-purpose]]. What does having the
endorsement on your certificate signify? OEE's own answer — that you know the content in the
standards — is a claim a cheaper instrument could support.

A complication noted in the room: higher education institutions may design their educator
preparation programmes as they wish and are not aligned across programmes, which is precisely
why transcript review is imperfect and why the endorsement test exists at all.
