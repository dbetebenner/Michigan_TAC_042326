**State as of April 2026:** OEAA has an identified problem, a failed vendor exploration, a
candidate replacement method, committee endorsement to pursue it, and a federal-plan blocker.

## The issue

An Adequate Growth Percentile is the Student Growth Percentile a student must consistently
meet or exceed to reach proficiency within a set timeframe. Michigan's content-area timeframe
is one to three years, capped at three for reliability reasons and Michigan legislative policy.
English Learner proficiency runs one to five years, capped at five on research and federal
policy grounds.

The cap is where the trouble is. A grade 11 student has one year of runway. The same
proficiency gap that yields a modest target in grade 4 yields an extreme one in grade 11 — and
ELA shows a larger average AGP gap than mathematics.

The consequence is that Michigan's accountability index overwhelmingly identifies high
schools. OEAA's read is that this reflects a K–12 district-wide instructional problem surfacing
late, not a high-school-specific failure. Current research holds that a school needs five to
seven years to meet growth; Michigan allows three at most.

The model is working as designed. OEAA is questioning the design.

## Where the committee landed

The committee accepted the technical fix and then argued with the premise.

On method it endorsed the [[nearest-grade-average-method]] —
[[rec-growth-pursue-nearest-grade-average]].

On the target itself it pushed **up**, not down. Adequate growth should be set above the 65th
percentile ([[rec-growth-adequate-target-above-65]]), on the reasoning that a student behind
grade level needs more than a year's learning in a year. For low-achieving schools it noted
that the 60th percentile is already adequate and — more pointedly — that "the norm actually
tells you more than the growth."

OEAA's stated reluctance is that raw numbers discourage, because the growth they imply cannot
be achieved. The committee's answer was that a target which cannot be achieved is a reporting
problem, not a reason to lower the bar.

Underneath sits a disagreement about purpose. The AGP was, in OEAA's own account, "an attempt
to communicate the urgency to make improvements." The committee would "rather see a system
that supports all kids" ([[rec-growth-support-all-students]]) and holds that "schools should
not be focusing on proficiency; their goal is to produce and maximize growth."

## What is not known

Whether AGPs are used, and used well. The committee asked for research
([[rec-growth-research-how-agps-are-used]]) and for a survey of whether elementary and high
schools interpret the same data the same way ([[rec-growth-survey-interpretation-consistency]]).
Neither exists.
