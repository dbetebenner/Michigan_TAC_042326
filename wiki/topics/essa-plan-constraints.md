**State as of April 2026:** the binding constraint on growth model change. Understood, not
resolved.

## The constraint

The Adequate Growth Percentile as currently defined is written into Michigan's approved ESSA
plan. Changing it requires a **plan amendment** — described in the room as "a large
department-wide plan," not something OEAA can execute alone.

## The distinction OEAA drew

OEAA believes the [[nearest-grade-average-method]] change is a **technical update** within its
own authority, not a change to the AGP definition requiring amendment. That distinction is
what makes the proposal actionable at all.

It is a distinction OEAA asserted; the corpus contains no record of it being tested with the
department's federal programmes staff or with the U.S. Department of Education.

## The trend break

Because the change touches all grades, adoption would require an **announced break in trend**.
Every longitudinal comparison across the boundary year would be invalid, and that has to be
communicated before the fact rather than explained afterward.

The committee asked directly how OEAA would explain a standard change and a break in trend.
The honest answer was that OEAA "hasn't gotten that far in planning" and is still deciding
whether a new growth calculation should be done at all. Tracked as
[[q-growth-break-in-trend-communication]].

## Why this matters beyond growth

The same pattern — a technically sound improvement gated behind a federal plan amendment and a
trend break — will recur for any change to a reported accountability metric. It is the reason
[[m-step-subscore-reporting]] decisions have a spring deadline for the following year's
results, and it is the reason the committee's advice on both topics leans toward getting the
inference right before changing the machinery.
