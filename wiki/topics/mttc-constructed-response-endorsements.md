**State as of April 2026:** OEE proposed building constructed-response tests for optional
science and social studies endorsements. The committee advised against it and offered four
cheaper alternatives. Not resolved.

## What OEE proposed

Constructed-response item (CRI) test forms for optional disciplinary specialisation
endorsements. A candidate must pass the science or social studies test before taking the
additional endorsement test.

- Science endorsements: biology, chemistry, Earth and space science, physics, physical science
- Social studies endorsements: civics, economics, geography, history

Nine additional tests. OEE's stated difficulty is the specificity CRIs require, and it wanted
other options.

The standards themselves were developed from stakeholders, experienced educators, and educator
associations and groups.

## The committee's answer

Don't — [[rec-mttc-avoid-constructed-response-endorsements]]:

> This creates a lot of work, will be hard to do, and will be expensive; suggest that this
> isn't necessary.

Pearson's question of how many CRIs per test went unanswered because the committee rejected
the premise.

## The four alternatives

- [[rec-mttc-transcript-review-alternative]] — verify the candidate took coursework in the area
- [[rec-mttc-conjunctive-cut-scores]] — require a higher, conjunctive cut on the existing test's sub-areas
- [[rec-mttc-random-standard-prompt]] — randomly select a standard as an open-ended prompt, scored against a rubric
- [[rec-mttc-interview-guide-for-districts]] — give districts a guide to what to ask at interview

The third is the most developed. Pearson already has rubrics for other CRI items. The
committee noted that social studies standards are not all written as questions, so the task
would evaluate the skill of developing an argument — a skill that applies across all four
social studies fields, which is what makes one instrument serve four endorsements. Cut scores
would be set from the rubric. Scoring capacity was named as the obstacle.

A variant was raised and not pursued: performance items rather than constructed response — for
example, develop a lesson plan.

## The complication

Higher education institutions may design educator preparation programmes as they wish and are
not aligned across programmes. That is what weakens transcript review, and it is also part of
why an endorsement test exists.

OEE noted that Educator Preparation Programs are permitted to develop their own assessment and
propose it to the superintendent — an existing route that already tolerates non-comparability.

The National Board Certification test was raised as an existing instrument but is not aligned
to Michigan standards.

## The question that governs all of it

[[rec-mttc-define-endorsement-purpose]]. What does having the endorsement on a certificate
signify? The answer given in the room — that the holder knows the content in the standards —
is a low bar, and it is a bar that a conjunctive cut on an existing test could clear at a
fraction of the cost of nine new constructed-response instruments.

This is the same move the committee made in [[mttc-meaningfully-different-forms]]: define the
inference first, then choose the instrument that supports it.
