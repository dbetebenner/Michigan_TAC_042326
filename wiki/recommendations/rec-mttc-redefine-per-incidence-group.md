**The advice.** "You could have a different definition of meaningfully different for the four
groups." And: "With the higher fields, the percentage of acceptable overlap could be less (less
overlap for higher incidence)."

**Why.** OEE's working definition is a single overlap rule — one ceiling for overall items, a
slightly lower one for the scorable set — applied across four groups that differ by an order of
magnitude in annual volume. (The ceilings themselves are in the restricted layer.) The
rule exists because low-incidence fields cannot fill an item pool fast enough, but it is being
applied to high-incidence fields that can. The constraint that justifies the rule does not
apply where the rule does the most damage.

Higher-incidence fields accumulate examinees quickly, so they can support more distinct items
and should be held to a *lower* overlap ceiling. Low-incidence fields need the tolerance.

**What it replaces.** The rejected definition — see
[[mttc-meaningfully-different-forms]] — together with the committee's judgement that a handful
of forms produced over several years, each sharing most of its items with the others, cannot be
called meaningfully different.

**Status.** Open. OEE would need to re-open the working definition with [[pearson]].
