**The advice.** "We can either resist allowing them more information, (or determine) what method
can we use that will give the 'least bad' information." And: "You want to provide happy medium
(between) no information and inaccurate information from scale scores."

**Why.** This is the committee's framing decision for the whole session, and everything else it
said about subscores follows from it.

The committee's technical position is that subscore reporting from a deliberately unidimensional
test is close to indefensible — "seems detrimental to provide that information from something
you developed to not do that." But it did not recommend stopping. The field's demand is real,
the reporting already exists, and withdrawal is its own kind of failure.

So the committee named the choice explicitly and took the second option: given that Michigan
will report something, report the thing that is least wrong, and be honest about how wrong it is.
That honesty requirement is [[rec-subscore-report-certainty]] — it is what separates "least bad"
from "less obviously bad."

**Status.** Open as a standing posture rather than a discrete action.
