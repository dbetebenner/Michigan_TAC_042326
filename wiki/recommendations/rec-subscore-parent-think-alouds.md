**The advice.** "Biggest focus for reporting should be what parents want to know and see. Seek
information on how parents interpret current report or sample report and then determine what
needs to be changed."

Individual think-alouds or focus groups, asking: *What does this report tell you about this
student? What can you say about the student based upon this report?*

**Why.** The reframe that redirected the session. OEAA arrived asking which classification method
is more accurate. The committee's answer was that accuracy is not the binding constraint — the
binding constraint is what a parent takes away, and nobody has measured that.

The two suggested questions are well chosen. The first elicits what the reader believes the
report says; the second elicits what they believe they can *conclude*. The gap between them is
where over-interpretation lives, and over-interpretation is the entire risk of subscore reporting
from a unidimensional test.

**Already in motion.** OEAA is working to develop a survey to gather feedback. Think-alouds were
recommended in addition, not instead — a survey measures reactions to a report someone has already
made sense of; a think-aloud catches the sense-making.

**Status.** Open; survey in development.
