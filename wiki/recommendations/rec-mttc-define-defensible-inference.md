**The advice.** "OEE should try to find a substantive statement vs. a procedural one" to answer
three questions: *What can you infer when a student doesn't get a cut-score? What does
performance mean? Scores on the MTTC represent what?*

**Why.** Asked what inferences can be defended for each test, OEE's answer was that a candidate
passing at the minimum cut score can be certificated, which allows a district to interview
them. That describes what the score *permits*, not what it *means*. It is a description of
process, and it cannot be defended in the way a validity claim is defended.

This is the most consequential recommendation of Session 4 because everything else depends on
it. "Meaningfully different" has no criterion until the inference is defined — two forms are
meaningfully different if they support the same inference equally well, and an undefined
inference cannot adjudicate one overlap ceiling against another. The same gap governs
[[mttc-constructed-response-endorsements]]: OEE cannot decide whether a constructed-response
endorsement test is necessary without knowing what claim it would support.

**Status.** Open. No answer offered in the session.
