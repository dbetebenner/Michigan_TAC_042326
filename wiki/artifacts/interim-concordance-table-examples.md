Jennifer Paul (OEAA). Three pages, landscape. Two alternative drafts — "Example 1" and
"Example 2" — of a Smarter Balanced Interim ↔ M-STEP concordance table. This is the artifact
the committee gave layout feedback on in [[2026-04-smarter-balanced-interim]].

## What the table contains

ELA and Math side by side, grades 3–7, performance levels 1–4. Each cell gives the lowest and
highest Smarter Balanced scale score and the corresponding lowest and highest M-STEP scale
score. Grade 3 ELA, for example, maps SBAC 2112–2363 to M-STEP 1203–1279 at Performance
Level 1; grade 7 Math maps SBAC 2632–2777 to M-STEP 1716–1752 at Performance Level 4.

## How the two examples differ

Only in framing text. Example 1 leads with the worked example the minutes quote:

> As an example, a 3rd grade student scoring a 2112 on the Smarter Balanced Comprehensive
> Interim ELA test (Performance Level 1) could score a 1203 (Performance Level 1) on the
> M-STEP ELA assessment.

Example 2 opens more loosely — "roughly predictive of how a student may score" — and defers
the worked example to a second page.

Both carry the same caveat: "a concordance table such as this is not a perfect prediction."

## What the committee said about it

The word *could* drew a direct question — "What does 'could' mean?" — answered as "student
would most likely score, or something along those lines." That imprecision, in a document
whose entire job is to communicate a probabilistic mapping to non-psychometricians, is the
substance of [[rec-concordance-move-key-takeaway-to-top]] and
[[rec-concordance-publish-formula]].
